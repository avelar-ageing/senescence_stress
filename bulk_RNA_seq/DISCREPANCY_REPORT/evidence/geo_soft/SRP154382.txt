!Sample_growth_protocol_ch1 = Proliferating fibroblasts were grown in a complete medium (DMEM and 10% FBS) and harvested at 60-70% confluency. For 7-day contact inhibited (7dCI) fibroblasts, the cells were plated at a density of 5 x 105 cells per 10 cm dish on day 1. Fresh medium was added every 48 hours and the contact-inhibited cells were harvested on day 7. For serum-starved cells, the cells were plated in complete medium at density of 5 x 105 cells per 10 cm dish. Next day (day 1), the complete medium was replaced with low serum medium (DMEM with 0.1% FBS). The medium was replaced with fresh medium (low-serum) every 48 hours. The serum-starved cells were harvested on day 7.
!Sample_molecule_ch1 = total RNA
!Sample_extract_protocol_ch1 = Proliferating, 7dCI, and 7dSS fibroblasts were lysed using 1 mL of Trizol reagent (Life Technologies, Carlsbad, CA) per 10 cm plate for 5 minutes.  RNA was isolated from Trizol lysates following manufacturer instructions. RNA concentrations were determined using a Nanodrop Spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA). RNA quality was verified on a Bioanalyzer 2100 (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA) using reagents from the RNA Nano 6000 kit (Agilent Technologies).
!Sample_extract_protocol_ch1 = cDNA libraries containing fragments enriched for 3´ ends of transcripts were created with the Gnomegen RNA-Seq Library Preparation Kit for RNA profiling (Gnomegen, San Diego, CA). Libraries with a uniform size distribution between 200 and 1000 bp were subjected to gel size selection to enrich for 200 – 300 bp sized fragments. Single-end 140 bp reads were generated on an Illumina HiSeq 2000 Instrument. The sequencing reaction was run for 147 cycles.
!Sample_data_processing = Reads from polyadenylation site-enriched cDNA libraries were aligned to the human genome (hg19) using STAR aligner.
!Sample_data_processing = Reads were assigned to a polyadenylation site based on annotations in the PolyA_DB version (active on Sept 25, 2014).
!Sample_data_processing = Reads matching to polyadenylation sites were then used as input into the DEXSeq differential exon expression-testing algorithm. The different polyadenylation sites for each gene were treated as different exons for DEXSeq analysis.
!Sample_data_processing = Significant differences in polyadenylation site use were determined across three biological replicates of the proliferating, 7dSS, and 7dCI cells. Genes with significant differences in polyadenylation site use (q < 0.1) were reported.
